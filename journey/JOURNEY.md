# The journey — "I let AI build and run my YouTube channel"

Raw material for a future "build in public" video/channel. **Only real numbers go here** — no rounding up, no
projections presented as results. Newest weekly entry at the bottom.

## The setup (what's actually automated)
- **Claude** (Claude Code) writes scripts, researches facts (2 sources per number), draws every frame in Python
  (custom 1930s cartoon engine — no stock footage, no AI image generator), generates the voice (free TTS),
  synthesises the music and sound effects, renders, uploads and schedules.
- **Metricool** (free plan, 20 posts/month) publishes to YouTube; **vidIQ** helps spot trending topics.
- **GitHub** stores everything; a **weekly scheduled run every Sunday** makes and schedules the next week alone.
- **The human (owner):** picks direction, taste calls (style, topics, "this is boring"), posts TikTok manually,
  connects accounts, approves big changes.

## Timeline (days 1–3)
### Day 1 — Wed/Thu 15 Oct 2026: from idea to first post
- Idea: a monetised money-explainer channel using AI scripts + animation. Chose "money/economics explainers"
  (high-paying ad category, suits code animation).
- **Style went through 3 versions in one day** (see `assets/01–03`): flat motion graphics → retro print with a
  coin mascot "Penny" → 1930s rubber-hose cartoon on a vaudeville stage. Owner dropped the mascot ("don't really
  like having the mascot"), asked for the burger to be redrawn, the film wobble toned down, captions moved up.
- Pilot "Why does a Big Mac cost different prices around the world?" posted to TikTok ~10pm.

### Day 2 — Fri 16 Oct 2026: branding, a flop, and automation
- Named the channel **The Common Cents**; logo made in code.
- Big Mac TikTok: **1,037 views, 15 likes, 0 comments** in ~15 h — views but no conversation.
- Pivoted to **Malaysian** money topics. Made a petrol-subsidy video — owner watched it and said
  *"it doesn't seem interesting to me"*. Diagnosis: a list of numbers, no story, no sound. Rebuilt as a story
  (Myvi vs SUV), added original ragtime music + cartoon sound effects, cut 67 s → 46 s (`assets/04` vs `05`).
  First mix was too loud → turned down. Owner ended up skipping it anyway.
- Traffic-jam video posted to TikTok ~1pm: **a comment within 20 minutes** — ending with a question works.
- Moved the project to GitHub, connected Metricool + vidIQ, used trend research (Budget 2027 tax-relief explainers
  were going viral) to make batch 1: tax relief, RM100-in-2015 inflation, RM2,000 minimum wage.
- Set up the **weekly scheduled run**. Hit real-world limits: Metricool free = 20 posts/month; TikTok's Creator
  Rewards likely not available in Malaysia → **switched to YouTube-first**; @thecommoncents was taken on YouTube.
- Made the YouTube banner; Claude Code needed explicit permission rules before running fully hands-free.

## Lessons so far
1. Taste still matters: the AI made a factually fine video that was boring — the human caught it.
2. Story > statistics. Characters + a twist + a question beat a list of numbers.
3. Sound is half the video (music + sound effects), but keep it under the voice.
4. Free plans and platform rules shape the strategy more than the AI does.
